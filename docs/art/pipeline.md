# The vector pipeline

Path: [docs](../README.md) › [art](README.md) › **pipeline**
Parent: [README.md](README.md)
**Covers:** how a picture becomes 56 cut files: design units, the per-plate homography, the local frames the lattices are drawn in, the viewpoint geometry extracted from the tool, the previews and the tool defaults.

## Decisions

- **The pictures are generated by a script, not drawn or produced by an image model** (agent,
  2026-10-22). No image generator was available in the session; more to the point, cuttability
  (no wood islands, minimum widths) and the fit to the silhouette of the towers are constraints
  that an image cannot be made to meet after the fact, so the art is parametric and checked by
  construction. The compositions are hand-placed in the script in "design units" (pixels of a
  2048-wide picture) so the tuned sizes survive a change of output resolution.
- **The cuts are vector, written straight to DXF per plate; the tool's own DXF export is not used
  for these pictures** (owner, 2026-10-22: "the projected DXF needs to be cut into wood, it can't be
  pixelated", then "do what is needed to avoid the pixelization issue", then "can you make the
  generator vector?"). The tool rasterises every picture (raster or DXF alike) onto 2 mm cells
  and traces a 1 mm mask, so nothing fed to it comes out smooth; the only way out is to never
  rasterise. The generator's first version was raster (lattices computed per pixel, checks by
  morphology, headless Chrome rendering the figures) and was replaced the same day. The preview
  PNG is 4096 px on the long side and the tool's `PICTURE_MAX` was raised from 2048 to match (at
  2048 the apex tower from the bench got 3.5 mm per pixel; the old cap's reason is unknown,
  presumably memory).
  **Constrains.** The cut files for the cutter are `art/dxf/`, not the tool's export. Moving a tower
  invalidates them: re-extract `art/viewpoints.json` and regenerate.
- **Lattices are drawn in each plate's local millimetre frame, figures in the picture plane**
  (agent, 2026-10-22). A picture pixel covers ~0.6 mm on the near tip towers and ~1.7 mm on the far
  apex tower (from the bench), and the faces are seen at 45°, so horizontal and vertical scales
  differ. A lattice drawn in the picture plane would come out with cells and lanes of varying size
  on the plates; drawn in the plate's frame (X and Y scaled by that face's pixels per metre, from
  `art/viewpoints.json`) the lanes are LANE_MM everywhere and the cells as fine as the rules allow.
  The figures are polygons in the picture plane and are mapped exactly, so they stay in register
  across tower edges. The frame is the affine approximation of the homography at the plate; the
  mapping of the polygons is the exact homography.
- **A stroke's width comes from the finest plate that shows it** (agent, 2026-10-22). From behind,
  the side towers overlap the apex tower in the picture and a lane drawn there is cut on both;
  sizing it by the hidden, coarser plate made it 6 mm on the apex plate, which the thin-wood step
  then removed: that was the broken right side of the mushroom.
- **The design-plane render** (`art/<name>-design.png`, owner, 2026-10-22: "what I don't have is
  the image before you applied the tower borders constraints, it could be very telling") draws the
  composition as polygons in the picture plane with the tower outlines, before any border, base,
  contour or cuttability rule, so a defect can be told apart as design or plate pass. It showed
  the mushroom's gills were one 300 mm slab on the side plates (a fixed ray count from the focus;
  now banded rays) and that the bird's feathers were right in the design and wrecked on the plates.
- **The tool opens with the demo smiley, not these pictures** (owner, 2026-10-23: "leave the
  smiley faces for now"). They were made for the 2026-10-21 layout and the artist is drawing
  different art ([README.md](README.md)), so projecting them on the final layout would show cuts
  that will never exist. To look at one anyway, upload `art/<viewpoint>.png` in the tool's
  picture panel.
  **Changed 2026-10-23:** previously the four pictures were the tool's defaults (owner,
  2026-10-22: "make all 4 the default in the 3d demo"): on opening, the page fetched
  `art/<viewpoint>.png` and unticked the mirror box. Changed because the layout moved under the
  pictures and they are no longer the art.
- **Statue 2 has its own pictures** (owner's brief). `tools/embed_pictures.py` writes the four
  preview pictures into `layout/statue-layout.json` (not since 2026-10-23: the file is now the
  artist's final layout with the smiley, see [README.md](README.md)) and unticks "statue 2 uses statue 1's
  pictures", since the default mirrors statue 1
  ([../strips_and_power/statue_geometry/pictures.md](../strips_and_power/statue_geometry/pictures.md)). The tool then
  shows the plates in 3D and its island report is a second opinion on the 1 mm preview; the DXF
  files it exports from them are raster traces and are not the cut files.
- **The towers render is anti-aliased** (agent, 2026-10-22): `art/<name>-towers.png` is drawn at
  twice its size from the plates' cuts at 0.5 mm and downsampled, after a nearest-neighbour render
  read as pixelated at full zoom although the cuts are vectors.
- **`art/viewpoints.json` is extracted from the tool, not computed again** (agent, 2026-10-22):
  the tool's projection is the truth, and reimplementing it would drift. Regenerate it in the
  tool's console after any layout change (the geometry of the silhouette is what the
  compositions are placed on):

  ```js
  const D = window.statueDebug, r = x => Math.round(x*1e4)/1e4, out = {};
  for (const key of ['bench1','back1']) { const box = D.povBox(key, D.current, D.faces), pose = box.pose, [x0,y0,x1,y1] = box.full, bw = x1-x0, bh = y1-y0;
    out[key] = { aspect: r(bw/bh), eye_mm: key.startsWith('bench') ? D.state.eye : D.state.povs[key].eye, faces: D.faces.filter(f => f.povKey === key).map(f => {
      const P = c => { const [sx,sy] = D.projectPt(pose, c); return [r((sx-x0)/bw), r((sy-y0)/bh)]; };
      const a = P(f.worldAt(0, f.tower.h/2)), b = P(f.worldAt(1, f.tower.h/2)), c2 = P(f.worldAt(0.5, 0.2)), d2 = P(f.worldAt(0.5, f.tower.h-0.2));
      return { tower: f.tower.name, face: f.letter, h_mm: Math.round(f.tower.h*1000), corners: f.corners.map(P), unlit: [f.corners[0], f.corners[1]].map(c => P(c.clone().setY(D.state.unlit/1000))),
               u_per_m: r(Math.hypot(a[0]-b[0], a[1]-b[1]) / f.pw), v_per_m: r(Math.hypot(c2[0]-d2[0], c2[1]-d2[1]) / (f.tower.h-0.4)) }; }) }; }
  JSON.stringify(out)
  ```
  Statue 2's viewpoints see the same silhouette as statue 1's (mirrored layout), so bench2 and
  back2 use the bench1 and back1 geometry.
- **Where faces overlap in the picture, the nearer plate wins in the preview** (agent,
  2026-10-22). From behind, parts of the side towers hide behind the apex tower but share its
  picture pixels; each plate's cuts are computed from the polygons independently, so the hidden
  parts are cut correctly, and only the preview PNG has to choose.
